# NDPA core reference (shared by all NDPA skills) — v0.2.0

## 0. Sources, precedence and how to use this file
**Sources** (full text shipped in `source/` next to this file):
- **Act** — Nigeria Data Protection Act, 2023 (Act No. 37; Extraordinary Gazette No. 119, 1 July 2023). Text: `source/NDPA-2023.official.txt`.
- **GAID** — NDPC General Application and Implementation Directive 2025 (NDPC/NDP ACT-GAID/01/2025), issued 20 March 2025 and **in effect from 19 September 2025** (effective date per NDPC announcement as reported by Aelex and Afriwise; it is not stated in the PDF), with Schedules 1-10. Schedule 7 reproduces Guidance Notice NDPC/HQ/GN/VOL.03/B/24 (dated 19 December 2024). Text: `source/NDPC-GAID-2025.official.txt`.
- Other NDPC instruments (not distilled here): Guidance Notice on CPD for DPOs (NDPC/HQ/GN/VOL.05/26, 2026), DPCO code of conduct, public-sector circular. Updates: https://ndpc.gov.ng/resources/
- **NDPR 2019 no longer applies as a regulatory instrument.** GAID Art.3(3): "upon the issuance of the GAID" (20 March 2025) the Commission "shall cease to apply" the NDPR; in line with Act s.64 (transitional provisions), things done under the NDPR before then are unaffected. Act s.64(2)(f) is why the NDPR (a NITDA regulation) survived the Act: NITDA/Bureau regulations "continue in effect … until they expire or are repealed, replaced … or altered". Do not cite the NDPR for current obligations.

**Precedence:** the Act prevails over any other law on personal data (s.63) and over the GAID (GAID Art.3(2)). The GAID can be amended by later NDPC regulations or directives, which prevail over it (GAID Art.50). Where the GAID adds a duty the Act does not state (for example consent in GAID Art.18), report it as a GAID requirement, note the Act text, and tag the conclusion **Interpretive**.

**Tags used below:** **[Act]** statutory duty · **[GAID]** NDPC directive · **[practice]** good practice or inference, not a stated legal duty. Never present a [practice] item as law.

**Verification status (2026-10-02):** this file was rebuilt after reading the full GAID (Articles 1-52, Schedules 1, 3, 5-10; Schedules 2 and 4 by structure) and checking every Act citation against the Gazette text. Re-check fees, tiers and deadlines on ndpc.gov.ng before a client-facing conclusion. This is a compliance aid, not legal advice; anything marked VERIFY needs a lawyer or the NDPC.

## 1. Applicability
- [Act] s.2: processing by automated or non-automated means where the controller/processor is domiciled, resident or operating in Nigeria, processing occurs in Nigeria, or a non-Nigerian controller/processor processes data of subjects in Nigeria. [GAID] Art.1(4): protects persons in Nigeria regardless of nationality, data transferred to Nigeria, data in transit (obligation limited to confidentiality, integrity, availability), and Nigerian citizens abroad (limited). Art.8(2): "operating in Nigeria" includes targeting data subjects in Nigeria.
- [Act] s.3(1): purely personal or household processing is excluded (GAID Art.6 still expects respect for others' privacy).
- [Act] s.3(2): Part V obligations **other than ss.24, 25, 32 and 40** do not apply where processing is (a) by a competent authority for crime prevention/prosecution, (b) public-health emergency, (c) national security, (d) publication in the public interest for journalism, educational, artistic or literary purposes (to the extent incompatible), or **(e) necessary for the establishment, exercise or defence of legal claims, in court or in administrative or out-of-court procedure**. [GAID] Art.5(2): exempt processing is still bound by ss.24, 25, 32, 40 **and Part VI (data subject rights)**; Part VII security (s.39) is outside Part V and still applies. Relevance: much of a law firm's matter processing may fall under (e); billing, HR, marketing, portal accounts and analytics do not. VERIFY scope per processing activity.
- Roles: **controller** determines purposes and means; **processor** processes on a controller's (or another processor's) behalf (s.65). One entity can be both for different datasets. An IT services firm administering a client's systems is usually the client's processor.

## 2. "Major importance" (MI) — drives registration, DPO and audit-return duties
[Act] s.65 defines MI by thresholds the NDPC prescribes or by designation. [GAID] Art.8-9 and Schedule 7:

**Gate (Sch.7 para 1(1); cite limbs as para 1(1)(a), (b), (c)):** keeps or has access to a filing system (analogue or digital) for personal data **and** any one of: (a) processes personal data of **more than 200 data subjects in 6 months**; (b) carries out **commercial ICT services on any digital device with storage capacity for personal data that belongs to another individual**; (c) operates in a listed sector — aviation, communication, education, electric power, export and import, financial, health, hospitality, insurance, oil and gas, tourism, e-commerce, public service. Purely personal/household processing is not caught (para 1(2)).

**Tiers (Sch.7 paras 2-3):** a body is categorised by **named type** or by **volume**, or by any four of the tier's listed factors. **Named type and volume in conflict** (e.g. a secondary school, named OHL, with over 1,000 data subjects): the text favours the named type. Para 3(1)(d) puts organisations over 1,000 in EHL "apart from those mentioned in sub-paragraph 3(1)(e)", which is the OHL named list (schools, training providers, primary health centres, labs, small hotels, sensitive-data processors). Read literally, a named OHL type stays OHL whatever its volume. The cross-references in paras 3(1)(b), (d), (f) look misnumbered ((f) points to a non-existent 3(1)(g)), so lead with the named-type answer, mention the volume reading as a risk, and tag **Interpretive**.
| Tier | Registration fee | Named types | Volume (data subjects in 6 months) |
|---|---|---|---|
| UHL ultra-high | ₦250,000 | commercial banks (national/regional), telcos, insurers, multinationals, electricity distribution companies, oil and gas, public social-media app developers/proprietors, public e-mail app developers/proprietors, communication-device manufacturers, payment gateways, fintechs | over 5,000 |
| EHL extra-high | ₦100,000 | MDAs, microfinance banks, higher institutions, secondary/tertiary hospitals, mortgage banks | over 1,000 and under 5,000 |
| OHL ordinary-high | ₦10,000 | primary and secondary schools, corporate training providers, primary health centres, independent medical labs, hotels/guest houses under 50 suites, processors of sensitive data of more than 200 data subjects for commercial purposes | over 200 and under 1,000 |

- **Not MI (Sch.7 para 4)** — three separate tests: (a) traders/artisans who do not pass personal data on as a business object to other controllers/processors; (b) traders with fewer than 15 employees, or artisans with no filing system beyond routine contacts, receipts, addresses and e-mails; (c) informal communities of friends/professionals on social media. Para 4(b) sits uneasily with the sector gate: a small online shop is in a listed sector (e-commerce, para 1(1)(c)) but may be a trader with fewer than 15 employees. The text does not say which wins; tag **Interpretive** and advise registering if in doubt.
- **Exempt from registration (Sch.7 para 6, under s.44(6)):** community-based associations, faith-based organisations, foreign embassies/high commissions, judicial or adjudicatory bodies, multigovernmental organisations.
- **Processor fee (Sch.7 para 7):** a UHL controller pays ₦5,000 per processor engaged in a 12-month period (not again for a replacement processor within 12 months; not for an OHL processor whose renewal it pays).
- **Not MI does not mean exempt:** s.39 security, s.40 breach notification, s.27 notice, Part VI rights, s.28 DPIA where triggered, s.29 processor agreements and the GAID Art.7 measures (privacy policy, annual compliance audit, training) apply to every controller. MI status adds registration, the statutory DPO and audit-return filing.
- **Registration:** [Act] s.44(1) within six months after the Act's commencement **or on becoming** MI (no grace period for a new entrant); s.44(3) notify significant changes within 60 days; s.44(2) lists the registration contents. [GAID] Art.9: UHL/EHL register once then file annual audit returns; OHL renews registration annually and files no audit return.
- **Audit-return (CAR) fees (GAID Sch.10, Art.10(10)):** UHL ₦1,000,000 (50,000+ subjects) / ₦750,000 (25,000-49,999) / ₦500,000 (below 25,000); EHL ₦250,000 (10,000+) / ₦200,000 (middle band, printed "5,000-2,500"; VERIFY) / ₦100,000 (below 2,500). Late filing adds 50% of the fee (Art.10(9)).

## 4. Enforcement exposure
- [Act] ss.46-47: complaints and own-initiative investigations; compliance orders (warning, compliance requirement, cease and desist).
- [Act] s.48: after investigation, remedy, compensation, account of profits, penalty or remedial fee. Cap: MI = greater of ₦10,000,000 or 2% of prior-year annual gross revenue; non-MI = greater of ₦2,000,000 or 2% (s.48(3)-(5)). Factors (s.48(6)): (a) nature, gravity, duration; (b) purpose of processing; (c) number of subjects; (d) damage and mitigation; (e) intent or negligence; (f) cooperation; (g) types of data.
- [Act] s.49: failing to comply with a s.47 **compliance order** is an offence: fine up to the same caps and/or up to one year's imprisonment. This is the Act's main offence.
- [Act] s.50: judicial review within 30 days of an order. s.51: data subjects may sue for damages.
- [Act] s.53: **where an offence is committed** by a body corporate or firm, its principal officers are deemed culpable unless they prove no consent or connivance **and** diligence to prevent it (s.53(1)); controllers and processors are vicariously liable for agents and employees acting in the business (s.53(2)). s.53(1) attaches to offences (s.49), not to s.48 administrative penalties.
- [GAID] Art.28(6): failure to conduct a DPIA may lead to restriction of platforms. Art.48: registration, CAR, DPIA and approved transfer instruments show good faith but are not a defence to a specific violation.
